Iko

IKO Ramen library is an a convivence store offering a wide assortment of instant ramen and other Asian snacks and drinks and as well as the possibility to prepare instant ramen on the spot across multiple locations in Riga.

They reached out initially for logo and signage design, that evolved into a longstanding collaboration developing IKO’s design language and brand through various design assets.

Brand design Packaging Print and advertisment

Client

IKO Latvia

Role

Designer

Timeline

Roughly April 2025 – March 2026

Deliverables

Branding, packaging, signage, print materials

Challenge

Plan and design various branding and interior design assets. Oversee printing, setup and implementation.

Impact

A signage system evolved and scaled across multiple locations.

Branding

Context

The brand identity is meant to reflect it’s ties to Asian cultures and their pop-culture. Taking inspiration from K-pop, anime and internet culture, the brand emulates elements of a “cute” aesthetic, particularly seen in the brands mascot capibara. Scalability is retained through the accent red allowing a more restrained designs for signage and more practical needs.

Key design decisions

The logo in particular was meant to reflect Asian influence- the vertical composition reminds of Japanese signage stamps, vertical letters- of hangul (the Korean writing system), the swirl is reminiscent of the swirl found in Japanese ramen topping fish cakes.

The colour palette was purposefully kept minimalistic- with white and a saturated red. It would allow more flexibility to develop the colour story and brand identity further in more serious B2B or more casual B2C contexts.

Packaging

Context

As a part of brand identity there was 5 different food packaging labels. The labels are all designed in a saturated and illustrated style keeping inline with other illustrative components of the brand style.

Key design decisions

To differentiate the 4 kimbap kinds each label was given a different accent colour as well as illustration of the namesake ingredient. As per the clients request the ingredients list is shown on the other half of the label.

Signage and interior print

Context

The added ability to buy it through self-service checkout, but also cook it through self-service ramen making machines, meant that the signage needed to allow the customer to navigate through the customer experience without a hitch.

While the initial design included more extensive signage, it was ultimately decided to streamline the information architecture and focus on essential elements to reduce cognitive load on the customer. In this case study, I focus more on how signage is split across the space (the more valuable part, in my opinion).

Navigational signage

Navigational signage, like floor decals, help users to move between different parts of the store in the correct order. They are mainly placed below eyesight line.

Warning labels

Warning labels concern the cooking area. Displayed mainly on electronics (ramen cooking machines, microwave) and on a red background, they caution against misuse of equipment and potential injury. Optimizing the design system, warning labels were made more prominent, while sticking to lighter backgrounds elsewhere.

Instructional signage

The bigger instructional panels are made more legible on a white background in the cooking zone to differentiate them from warning labels and to improve legibility.
